## 2. Where I hold, with operating evidence

### H1: Caio's confirmation must be written on the job (next cycle, not this one)

- **Grok's own day flow, step 5:** "On-site truth is Caio … He confirms
  arrival and completion."
- **Grok's spine:** no row writes that confirmation anywhere.
- **Operating consequence:** at 6pm the dispatch board (VA-106) can show crew
  and address, but it *can't* show "not yet confirmed by Caio". So the ACS
  one-query ("which jobs tomorrow are missing crew, access, or confirmation?")
  is only two-thirds answered from the phone. The last third lives in
  Bailey's head and his call log. That's the "side spreadsheet" Grok's own
  march test forbids ("without a side spreadsheet").
- **What it is not:** a new rail, a bot, a send, or a live
  en-route/on-site state machine (I already cut Fable's version back for the
  same reason). It's a chip and a note that Bailey or Blaze enters *after*
  the Continuity call.
- **This cycle:** nothing. It waits behind VA-106 in the next cycle. Question
  B1 for Bailey.

### H2: Share modes must not wait on Madeline's sheet

- **Cycle lock:** share modes need rows 1 and 3. The lock doesn't list row 5
  (the Kimi harvest) as a prerequisite.
- **Grok's row 6:** it writes "the status field from item 5".
- **The risk:** if Bailey hasn't shared Madeline's sheet by the time rows 1
  and 3 prove, the builder waits on the Commander's inbox. That stalls the
  train for a document, not for a defect.
- **Operating fix:** the share-mode "finish" writes the review outcome that
  version-bound approval rounds already carry (finished, approved, or changes
  requested, on that version; `20260922073000_version_bound_approval_rounds.sql`).
  When row 5 lands, Madeline's column names map onto those outcomes in row 8.
  The control is still real and the write is still real. Nothing is an
  invented board.

### H3: The one-client pilot is right, but the Excel sheet dies at the cross-client home

- **I adopt Grok's one-client pilot** (row 8, per-deliverable status on the
  existing project).
- **But Madeline's sheet is a cross-client view:** every client and every
  deliverable on one screen. That's why it exists.
- **Operating consequence:** status that lives only on each project page means
  Madeline opens N project pages to rebuild what one sheet shows her. She'll
  keep the sheet.
- **So the home isn't P3 polish.** The cross-client "waiting on whom" home
  (row 10) is the moment north star #1 is actually met, and it closes the nav
  ledger row. It comes next cycle, right after the pilot and close-out, not
  after money and AI.

### H4: Money on the job, authority in CCO OS

- **Grok says:** "Proposal is lines and a total on that same project … Money
  is not a side file."
- **The repo has already built that seam the other way:**
  `20260812000000_commercial_handoff_fields.sql` puts `commercial_total_cents`
  and the estimate references *on the CVP project*. It also says "Co-VideoPro
  never mutates them — CCO OS remains the commercial authority". There's no
  proposals API route in CVP.
- **Operating consequence of a CVP-owned proposal/invoice:** two places where a
  price can change, so the invoice disagrees with the estimate, and Bailey
  reconciles money by hand, which is exactly the side file Grok wants gone.
- **Lock:** money *shows on the CVP job* (Grok's surface, Fable's cockpit
  sections). CCO OS writes it, and it moves only when Bailey says bill (G15).

### H5: What "leave Wipster hosting" means for the share-mode Land

- **Grok's row 6:** "Hosting stays at Wipster."
- **CVP already hosts review media itself:** the September commits made CCNAS
  publication SMB-safe, deferred scans, and enqueued derivatives. A guest
  reviewing in CVP share modes is watching a CCNAS-published version, not a
  Wipster file.
- **The risk:** read literally, "hosting stays at Wipster" could send Reel to
  embed Wipster-hosted media in the CVP review route.
- **Clarified lock:** *existing* Wipster projects and archives stay on Wipster,
  with no migration. *New* review rounds run on CVP-hosted versions. I think
  this is what both seats mean. It's question B4 so Bailey closes it in one
  word.
