# PART ONE — WHAT EXISTS

## 1. The landscape, in one breath

Content Co-op has not been building one product. It has been building **one product nine times**, in nine dialects, and leaving each draft on the floor. The drafts are astonishingly good. Scattered across the repos and the archive there is: a Premiere-grade video editor with a transcript-driven soundbite engine; a writing studio that turns its entire app into a warm paper desk the moment you start to write; a client review room that looks like a mature Frame.io competitor and white-labels itself per client; a Bloomberg-terminal money console where quotes are edited like spreadsheet cells; a proposal studio that renders a cinematic cover live as you type prices; a screenplay editor that simulates a US-Letter page with industry-indented dialogue; a brief wizard that ends by printing a production plan with a live price range; and a family of quote, invoice, and proposal PDFs that range from competent procurement documents to a dark cinematic cover that turns a quote into a movie poster.

Nothing here needs inventing. Everything here needs *choosing*.

## 3. The seven dialects at war

Right now the universe speaks seven visual languages at once. The audit pins each one down exactly:

1. **Cream Editorial** — the public voice. Warm cream `#f0ebe0`, parchment `#faf6ef`, sand hairlines `#d8cfc0`, deep navy ink `#0b1928`, sapphire `#1e4d8c`, periwinkle `#b3c8f0`, copper `#c4722a`. Fraunces serif headlines with italic accent words, Plus Jakarta Sans body, pill buttons, generous air. *This is the brand's soul.*
2. **Dark Cinema** — the product voice. Near-black navy `#070f1c`→`#121c2b`, ice ink `#edf3ff`, electric sapphire `#4c8ef5`. Glass panels, ambient video, glowing dots. *This is the brand's stage.*
3. **Green Phosphor Terminal** — the operator voice. `#07111f` with radial green glow, a single `#4ade80` accent, 28-pixel rows, micro-type down to 0.42rem, tabular numerals, twelve status colors. Bloomberg for a two-business agency. *This is the brand's cockpit.*
4. **Paper Desk** — the deep-work voice. Canvas `#eae2d3`, translucent cream cards, rust signal `#b55233`, moss `#5e6a52`, brown-tinted shadows, Fraunces titles. Appears only where a human does slow, thoughtful work. *This is the brand's study.*
5. **Royal Light Cockpit** — the newest voice. Cool gray canvas `#f5f7fa`, white cards, ink navy `#18223e`, royal blue `#145bb8`, Manrope display, 8px corners, dark media islands. *This is where Co-VideoPro is heading.*
6. **Four-Color Identity** — the logo voice. Red clapperboard, blue filmstrip, green loop, yellow plane. Lives on the welcome hero and nowhere else. *This is the brand's flag — flown outside, never inside.*
7. **Dead dialects** — lime `#b9ff77` + Space Grotesk ("Green is dead," the brand book decrees), the Jobber-Atlantis light admin kit, the dark Wipster black-and-green. *These are fossils. Admire, then bury.*

# PART TWO — THE DOCTRINE

Ten laws that govern every surface below. Each law is already proven somewhere in the audit; the doctrine simply promotes the best local decisions to universal ones.

**1. One brand, three altitudes.** Public surfaces wear Cream Editorial. Working surfaces wear Royal Light Cockpit with Dark Cinema islands where media plays. Deep-focus surfaces (the writing desk, the edit bay) may invert entirely — cream in a dark app — because costume-change is a proven way to say "you are somewhere else now." The operator console keeps its terminal density but re-tunes its accent from green to the brand's signal system.

**2. Color is state, never decoration.** Lifecycle colors are cool and fixed — blue for in-progress, amber for attention, red for risk, green for done and money — and warm colors are reserved for moments that demand a decision. The four-color logo stays at the door. Inside, the Webster stage-signal grammar applies: amber = intake, blue = development, red = production pressure, green = delivered.

**3. Every number is a door.** A count on a card lands you on the exact rows it counts, with the repair verb already in hand. "3 open comments" opens those three comments. "$12,750 outstanding" opens that invoice. No number leads to a report page.

**4. Density with calm.** The terminal console proves 28-pixel rows and 0.42rem labels can feel serene when the palette is disciplined and only one accent glows. Density is a typographic achievement, not a spatial one.

**5. The stage is always dark; the desk is always light.** Media — video, filmstrips, waveforms — sits on near-black islands wherever it appears, for contrast and respect. Reading, writing, and pricing sit on white or cream. The two never mix within a panel.

**6. Nothing overwrites without a click.** Every AI pass, every template swap, every restore renders as a preview with Replace / Insert / Append / Copy as explicit verbs. The paper-desk editor's "nothing ever overwrites the draft without a click" becomes universal law.

**7. Governance is visible, not hidden.** Draft → In review → Approved chains, immutable revision history, approval stairs with named steps, honesty pills on anything not yet real. The Proposal Studio's "UNISSUED DRAFT" amber badge and the control center's "Backend required" pills are the pattern: beautiful, but never lying.

**8. Documents are surfaces too.** A quote PDF, an invoice, and a proposal are designed screens that happen to print. They obey the same tokens, the same type, the same total bar. The dark cinematic cover grammar is canon.

**9. Every surface answers three questions at a glance.** What is this? What state is it in? What can I do next? The verb is always visible, always singular-primary, always labeled with a word a client would use.

**10. Cherry-pick, never average.** Where two versions of a surface exist, take the better one whole. Do not blend. The audit's registry (Part Four) names the winner for every surface.

## SURFACE P — THE DOCUMENTS (quote, invoice, proposal as print)

**Mood:** the Gen-3 dark cinematic system — the superseded PDFs that were too good to stay superseded. This is canon.

**The quote cover:** a near-black page. A hairline blue rule. Tracked small-cap chrome. An enormous condensed grotesque headline in white, with one electric-blue line. A gray synopsis. Outlined meta chips ("ONE FILM — 5–8 MIN TARGET"). A four-cell ISSUE / VALID / PAYMENT / QUOTE grid. FROM / BILL TO. And the masterstroke: an **inverted white total bar** anchoring the foot of the page — black condensed "TOTAL INVESTMENT — CHAPTER ONE" on the left, the huge blue price on the right. It turns a quote into a movie poster while keeping every procurement element legible.

**Interior pages:** white editorial paper. Blue "01 · DELIVERABLE" eyebrows, black condensed headlines under blue rules, two-column body, tint callouts with blue spines, and the effort-accounting table — numbered items with hours and amounts, hour-by-hour sub-rows, one highlighted key row, a 2px ink total rule.

**The invoice one-pager:** a massive condensed "INVOICE" masthead with the number in blue, a meta strip (TERMS / AGAINST QUOTE / STAGE "1 of 2 · Commissioning"), one numbered line item, an ink-black **TOTAL DUE NOW** band, and the killer feature — a **"TWO WAYS TO PAY"** card: left, "CARD — SECURE LINK" with the Stripe URL in a light-blue chip; right, "SDI / PO ROUTING" for procurement. Two rails: one for the warm contact, one for the purchasing department.

**The proposal:** the nine-page rhythm — cover → about → scope → approach → package ladder → timeline → investment → terms → acceptance. White pages, 54pt sapphire uppercase cover titles with a giant aperture watermark bleeding off the sheet, navy OPTION A/B/C badges each with a "Best for" line, pricing cards with mini line-item boxes, a cost-breakdown table, an Optional Add-Ons rate card, two-column legal, and an acceptance page with checkbox scope selection and dual signature blocks under charcoal header bars. Companions already designed and loved: the one-page **Roadmap** ("FROM YES TO DELIVERED FILM" — five numbered stages, each with a "WE NEED FROM YOU" panel), and the **manifesto** ("WHAT MAKES IT GOOD" — giant argument, red-vs-blue comparison table, six-cell craft grid).

**House rules for all documents:** proposals stay white and sans (no cream, no Fraunces — procurement restraint); quotes may wear the dark cinematic cover; the Field Paper landscape variant (full-bleed photography, green-on-dark, PT Serif italic) is reserved for treatments and pitches; every document ends with its folio and "Page N of NN."
